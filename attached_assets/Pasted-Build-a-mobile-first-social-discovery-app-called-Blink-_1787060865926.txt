Build a mobile-first social discovery app called “Blink”.

Blink is a hyperlocal social app where people discover what is happening around them right now — nearby people, live meetups, communities, events, interesting places, and things to do.

Core experience:

1. DISCOVER
Create a Discover screen with personalized discovery modes:
- For You
- Foodie
- Fitness
- Geeks
- Adventure
- Entertainment
- Explore
- Hidden Gems

These modes should NOT hard-filter the content. They should change the ranking/prioritization. Matching people, meetups and places should move toward the top, while non-matching content remains visible below.

Discover should have separate sections such as:
- People Nearby
- Happening Now
- Upcoming Events
- Nearby Places
- Collections
- Explore Your City

2. MAP
Create a live map centered around the user's location.

Map filters are different from Discover filters and should focus on social activity:
- Live Now
- Meetups
- Events
- Communities
- Places For You
- Nearby

Live Now means actual social activity/meetups/events — ordinary restaurants or cafes should NOT appear simply because they have people nearby.

Map markers should visually prioritize relevant/active items while keeping other items visible.

3. PEOPLE
Users have profiles containing:
- Name
- Photo
- Bio
- Interests (up to 10)
- Current mood
- Current intent
- Approximate location
- Activity/availability

Current mood and interests should influence discovery ranking. For example, someone currently in a coding/work mood should be prioritized in Geeks, while someone interested in technology should also receive relevance even if their current mood differs.

4. MEETUPS
Users can create spontaneous flash meetups around their location.

Meetups should support:
- Title
- Description
- Location
- Intent
- Participants
- Host
- Time/status
- Live/active state

Meetups should generally receive higher social priority than ordinary places when relevant.

5. PLACES
Show nearby cafes, restaurants, parks, gyms, coworking spaces, entertainment venues, etc.

Places should be ranked using a combination of:
- Distance
- User interests
- Current mood
- Discovery mode
- Rating
- Social activity
- Active users/check-ins
- Meetups
- Time/context

6. COLLECTIONS + EXPLORE YOUR CITY
Create dynamic sections such as:
- Coffees under ₹300
- Best views nearby
- Study-friendly places
- Photography walks
- Hidden local spots
- Weekend brunch
- Nature
- Parks
- Food
- Shopping
- Nightlife
- Adventure
- History
- Events
- Wellness

These should use place category/type information plus names, descriptions, ratings, price, attributes and semantic classification rather than relying only on simple category matching.

7. RANKING PRINCIPLE
The most important product rule:

NEVER remove something just because it doesn't match a filter.

Filters are relevance/ranking controls, not restrictive filters.

Example:
If the user selects “Geeks”, technology-related people, meetups and places appear first, but foodies, parks, restaurants and other people remain visible lower in the results.

Do not show users numerical recommendation scores. The UI should simply change the ordering.

8. UI
Make the interface modern, clean and visually focused on nearby activity.

Prioritize:
- Map
- People
- Live meetups
- Discovery cards
- Places
- Collections

Use smooth but subtle interactions. Do not expose internal ranking/scoring systems.

Build a functional prototype with realistic mock data and working navigation/interactions. Keep the architecture clean so a real backend/database and location services can later replace the mock data.
